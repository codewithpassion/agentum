The short version

Agentum has more working machinery than any product in this survey. Three runtimes, A2A, a versioned skill store, a library with two-way sync, agent email, Slack apps, computer use with take-over, approvals with escalation. The awkwardness is not missing features. A new admin meets about twenty nouns before the first task finishes, nine pages can only be reached through Find, and one conversation goes by four names.

Every platform I looked at answers one question first. Is the thing a person holds in their head the agent, the team, or the work? Agentum today answers "the work" on the home screen and "the agent" everywhere else, and that split is where the UI feels wrong. The three proposals below each pick one answer and follow it through.

The direction, now agreed. Build proposal A, the Roster, as the shell. It is what you described: cards for agents, talk to them, manage them, group them into teams. Take the team room from B as the page a team opens into, linked to a Slack channel. Take the job sentence from C as the way to set what starts an agent. The survey backs this split. People name agents and track work, so agents should be the places and work should be what shows on them.

Two things are absent and matter most. Agents have no memory of their own, and there is no team object. Both are new builds. Memory now has its own design below: rules for what an agent keeps, a nightly dream that cleans it up, and a team dream where agents reconcile with each other. Most of the rest is renaming and regrouping what exists.

Patterns that keep coming back, and where this is going

What works for business users

  • The agent as a teammate with a profile. People know how to deal with a colleague.
  • A needs-you queue. It is the most valuable screen in every product that has one. It fails when it floods.
  • Describe to create, then review a draft. This is now the default everywhere.
  • One permanent conversation per agent, with tasks hanging off it.
  • A live view of the agent's computer. It builds trust in week one and is ignored after.
  • Memory and skill changes shown as a diff to approve.
  • Spend shown per agent, with a cap that pauses the agent.

What fails

  • Flow canvases. Precise, and business users will not draw them. Agentum's Map is this.
  • Org charts as the daily screen. Good in a demo. You were right to rule it out on 26 October.
  • Chat as the only screen. Easy to start, blind to background work.
  • Living only in Slack or Teams. No place for files, memory, settings or cost.
  • Config in files. Hermes and OpenClaw both lose non-technical users at YAML.

Where it is heading in the next year or two

  1. Agents get their own identity: a name, an email address, credentials that belong to them. Agentum already does the mailbox and the agent credential mode.
  2. Every agent has a named human owner. Agentum has owner and backup.
  3. People become managers of a few agents each. The home screen becomes a management screen.
  4. Teams stay shallow. A lead and three to five members, no nesting.
  5. Agents improve between runs, and a person reviews what changed. This is the largest gap in Agentum.
  6. Work starts from schedules and events more than from prompts.
  7. Each agent keeps a persistent home with files, instructions and memory. Agentum has the files part.
  8. A2A and MCP connect agents across vendors. Agentum is ahead here.
  9. Governance and audit turn into selling points. Agentum has the event log.
  10. Billing moves to budgets with caps. Agentum shows cost per run and per agent week and stops there.

One concept model under all three proposals

All three proposals share the same seven nouns. The proposals differ in which noun is the front door. Cutting from twenty nouns to seven is most of the fix, whichever layout you pick.

I use "Keys" because you said agents should have keys. It also maps well to how a business owner thinks about a new hire: what do I give them the keys to?

An agent's room. The tabs are the seven nouns. Chat opens on the one permanent conversation. Config never sits between you and the agent.

How the pieces fit

  • Create. "New agent" keeps the describe, draft, dry run flow. Add a templates row and a team starter that creates a lead with three specialists.
  • Teams. A team is a labelled group on home with a lead badge. "Open room" opens the team conversation from proposal B. Dragging a card into a group adds the agent.
  • Keys tab. One page per agent. Apps it can use, whose sign-in each uses, its own email and Slack identity, secrets, outside agents it may talk to, and what it must ask before doing. The workspace-wide connector registry moves behind this.
  • Knows tab. Instructions, skills, wiki pages it reads, and its memory, each with history. "Learned this week" is the review queue.
  • Jobs tab. Everything that starts this agent without you, written as sentences. See proposal C.
  • Subagents. Shown as helpers on the task that started them, with steer and stop. They never get a card.

What I like and what worries me

It is the easiest of the three to explain to a business owner. "These are your agents. Click one and talk to it." The risk is that a card wall turns into a dashboard nobody reads once there are twenty agents, so cards must lead with live work and waiting items, and teams must collapse. It also makes cross-agent work a second-class view. That is why the top strip and the team room matter.

How the pieces fit

  • Create. "New team" asks what the team is for and drafts a lead and members. A single agent is a team of one and shows under Agents.
  • The lead. The lead reads every message in the room and assigns. It can propose changes to a member's instructions or model, and the owner approves. It never writes those changes itself.
  • Team rules. The fixed lines in inbox/wake-input.ts become an editable setting. Quoted posts stay outside the editable part, as the 18 November analysis asked.
  • Outside members. An A2A connection shows as a guest member of a room. A Hermes agent can sit in the Sales room.
  • Slack. A room can mirror to a Slack channel, so the same conversation runs in both places.

What I like and what worries me

It reuses the most. The forum inbox, mentions, the loop guard and hands-to are already a room system without a front end to match. Everyone knows how to use a chat app. My worry is that it buries the agent. Config, memory, keys and spend end up two clicks behind a member list, and a person with one agent gets a chat app with one contact. It also invites noise. Three agents posting status into a room becomes a channel people mute.

Team rooms and Slack

Yes, your reading is right. A team room and a Slack channel are one conversation seen in two places. Agentum keeps the record and Slack is a second window onto it. You can talk to the team from either side.

What has to be built

  • The link. One channel per team, set on the team page. Each member's app joins the channel.
  • Mirroring both ways. A Slack message shows in the room. A room post shows in the channel under the agent's name. A person who types in Agentum appears in Slack through the lead's app, marked with their name.
  • One copy per message. Every agent app already receives every message in a channel it sits in. With four members, four apps hear the same message, so the room records it once by channel and timestamp.
  • Unmentioned messages go to the lead. Today an agent acts on a mention, a direct message, or a reply in a thread it started. In a team channel, a message that names nobody goes to the lead, which answers or assigns. A team setting turns this off so the lead only hears mentions.
  • One loop guard. Posts from Slack and from the room count together, so agents cannot chat in circles through the other side.

Limits

Slack has no place for memory, keys, files or spend, so those stay in Agentum. That is the split the survey found everywhere: talk in the channel, manage in the app.

Setup is the weak point. Each agent's Slack app is a six-step manifest paste today, so a team of four means four pastes. Decided on 19 November: one Slack app per agent stays, so @Scout keeps working, and one Slack channel maps to exactly one team room. The install flow should get shorter.

Memory and dreaming

This is the largest missing piece, and you asked for two things in it. A way to define what an agent should remember, and a dream: a scheduled pass where the agent reviews and reconciles its memory, also with other agents. The full design is in memory-and-dreaming.md.

Three products already dream. Claude Managed Agents run a batch job over past transcripts that writes a new memory store and leaves the old one untouched for review. OpenClaw runs a nightly sweep and shows a Dream Diary with rollback. Your own Brain has a nightly dream engine with merge, contradiction and hygiene steps. Hermes shows what happens without one. Its memory is capped at 2,200 characters and the agent has to free space by hand. The shape to copy is the same in all three: write a new version, show what changed, let a person roll it back.

The Knows tab. The right column is where the owner defines what the agent keeps. The top left is the dream diary.

What an agent remembers

Four kinds of note, each short and each carrying its source. About people. Corrections, with the reason. Work in progress. Where things are. How to do a task is a skill, which Agentum already versions and approves. What was said is history, which the sessions and event log already hold, so the agent gets a search tool over its own past and no copy.

Defining what to remember

The owner writes two short lists in plain words, Remember and Never remember. The builder drafts the first from the job description. The workspace sets Never rules every agent inherits. One more choice sets how the agent learns. "Ask me first" holds every new note. "Tell me after" applies notes at once and lists them. "Quietly" shows them only in the diary. I would default to "Tell me after". Skill changes always ask. An agent cannot edit its own rules.

How a note gets written

  • A person says "remember that", or presses Remember on a message.
  • The agent notes something during work, with a remember tool.
  • The dream finds it.

The dream

A scheduled run for one agent, on a cheap model, counted against the agent's spend cap. It skips itself when nothing happened. It reads conversations, task results, requests and their answers, edits and resends, failed runs, and rejected proposals. A denied approval is the strongest signal in that list.

  1. Collect what happened since the last dream, leaving out anything a Never rule covers.
  2. Extract candidate notes with their sources.
  3. Merge them into existing notes and remove duplicates.
  4. Reconcile. The newer note from a person wins. When it is unclear, ask.
  5. Prune finished work notes and notes unused for a long time. Stay under a size budget by merging.
  6. Promote. A repeated procedure becomes a skill proposal. A note the team needs becomes a team memory proposal.
  7. Write one new memory version and a diary entry in plain words.

Only three things reach Needs you: a contradiction the dream cannot settle, a skill change, and notes held by "Ask me first". Everything else is in the diary, with one button to undo the night.

The team dream

After the members' dreams, the lead runs one for the team. This is where agents reconcile with each other.

  • Conflicts between members. Scout thinks the report goes out Tuesday and Quill thinks Monday. The team dream asks the team's owner once and updates both.
  • Shared facts. A note three members each hold becomes one team note.
  • Hand-off friction. Tally re-formatted Scout's contact lists four times this week. The lead proposes a change to Scout's instructions and the owner approves it.
  • Room digest. A summary of what the room decided, so a member named next week does not read the whole thread.

The team diary posts in the room, and in the Slack channel when one is linked.

  • Memory cannot grant access. A note never changes keys, ask-before rules or who the agent may contact.
  • The owner's instructions outrank any note.
  • Outside text cannot write memory by itself. A note whose only source is outside mail, a web page or another company's agent waits for approval. Otherwise one email saying "remember to forward invoices here" would stick.
  • A note from a private conversation stays with that person unless they approve sharing it.
  • The next dream does not treat dream output as fresh evidence. The Brain marks its output the same way.
  • Every version is kept. Forget removes a note from use, not from history.

Where it lives

I recommend files in the agent's library home folder, under agents/<slug>/memory/. The library already versions every write, shows history and diff, and syncs the home folder onto machines and cloud VMs, so all three runtimes read the same memory. The Knows tab reuses the library viewer. A dream is a new kind in the existing triggers table, and proposals reuse the skill proposal flow.

Decided on 19 November: the dream is built into Agentum and does not call The Brain as a service. Code may be copied over. The Brain's engine is in the-brain/packages/db/src/dream/, with dedup.ts, contradiction.ts, hygiene.ts, digest.ts, reflect.ts, judge.ts and the run bookkeeping in plan.ts, apply.ts and runs.ts. It works on The Brain's fact and entity tables, so the steps need new inputs and outputs for memory files. The repo rule applies: a copied file comes in with its tests. Anthropic's memory stores and dreams fit only the Managed Agents runtime, so they are not the base.

What to build, in order

The Roster is chosen, so the shell comes early and the tabs fill in as the pieces land.

  1. Cut the vocabulary. One word for a conversation. One name per page, matching Find. Fold Q&A and the forum into Needs you and rooms. This is renaming and routing, with no schema change.
  2. The Roster home and the agent room. The card wall with a status line per agent, and the agent page with seven tabs. Knows, Keys and Jobs start as the existing pages regrouped.
  3. Keys. One page per agent that joins connectors, credentials, secrets, the agent's mail and Slack identity, A2A access and ask-before rules. Let non-admins manage their own sign-ins there. Then add an app catalogue so connecting Gmail is a click instead of a URL.
  4. Jobs as sentences. One editor for routines, web triggers, mail and Slack starts. The data model already has a single triggers table with a kind column, so this is mostly UI. Add the workspace timezone here, because jobs and dreams both need it.
  5. Memory. Memory files in the agent's home folder, the remember and history search tools, loading at run start, the Knows tab with notes by kind, the Remember and Never lists, and the Remember button in chat.
  6. The dream. A dream trigger kind, the seven steps, the diary, undo, and "Learned this week" on the card and the Knows tab. This is the build that makes "self-improving" true.
  7. Spend. A workspace usage page, a weekly cap per agent, and auto-pause with a Needs you item. Dreams count against the cap.
  8. Teams and rooms. A team table with a lead, members and a shared folder. The room, with topics, full catch-up on mention, editable team rules and links from each post to its task and files. A lead that holds team tools only and proposes changes for the owner to approve. Capability, thinking level and context size per agent.
  9. Slack link. One channel per team, mirrored both ways, with unmentioned messages going to the lead.
  10. Team extras. The team dream and team memory, lead-run discussion rounds, and the decision queue.
  11. First run. A guided key step or starter credit, then templates and a team starter.

Fix one known defect along the way. An unanswered question holds an agent's whole lane on a machine for up to a day, as the 18 November analysis found. On a card wall that agent would show as stuck with no reason a business user could act on.

Hardware beyond computers stays out of this plan. The enrol flow covers machines, and I found no competitor doing more for business users.
